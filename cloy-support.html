When it's more than sugar

  • If you take medicine for diabetes, talk to your doctor before you cut back on sugar: your dose may need to change.
  • If food, eating or your body feels hard to talk about, you don't have to handle it alone. In the US, the National Alliance for Eating Disorders helpline is free: 1-866-662-1235, or visit allianceforeatingdisorders.com. Elsewhere, ask your doctor about support where you live.
  • In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

Common questions

How do I restore my subscription on a new iPhone?

Sign in with the same Apple Account, open Cloy and tap Restore at the bottom of the Cloy Pro screen.

How do I cancel?

Open the iPhone Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions, and pick Cloy. Cancel at least 24 hours before the renewal date to avoid the next charge. Apple handles all payments and refunds.

How does the free trial work?

The yearly plan starts with 3 days free. If notifications are on, Cloy reminds you the day before the trial ends. You won't be charged if you cancel before the trial ends. The monthly plan has no trial.

Do I have to stop eating sugar on the first day?

No. Keep eating as usual while you read the chapters, one a day. The plan ends with your Last Bite in the final chapter.

I had a slip. Is my count gone?

No. A slip is noted, never a reset: your sugar-free days stay. Cloy asks what happened and what it really gave you, and if slips pile up it suggests a short chapter again.

The Ingredient X-Ray doesn't open.

It needs an iPhone XS or newer and access to the camera. Open the iPhone Settings app, tap Apps > Cloy and turn on Camera.

Where is my data? Can I move it to another phone?

Your answers, plan and progress stay on your iPhone, so we can't see, recover or move them. They are included in your iCloud or computer backup, so restoring a backup to a new iPhone brings them along.

My reminders don't arrive.

Open the iPhone Settings app, tap Notifications > Cloy and make sure notifications are allowed.

Is Cloy a medical treatment?

No. Cloy is a habit-change app, not medical advice or treatment. If you have diabetes or another health condition, are pregnant or take medication, talk to your doctor before changing what you eat.
